Assignment 1a/1b Questions

What were your expectations about your internship before you joined?

My expectations surrounding the internship position I was assuming were mostly technical, involving hands-on work with live and customer-facing infrastructure, IT and DevOps process handling and exposure to a real-world work environment. I often associated myself with being an active learner and participant in my team, balancing work, and execution of my existing knowledge alongside opportunities to learn and grow my skillset where possible.

What was the reality? How was it different from your expectation?

By considering my desire to continuously grow, my motivation to grow and embrace the experience grew alongside my responsibilities as a Junior DevOps engineer. I took on more responsibility alongside my peers, including the provisioning and management of critical infrastructure, overseeing deployments of applications and time-sensitive duties. My involvement in the company also grew, assisting with the running of important events such as company conferences and team-building days. By embracing these opportunities, I was able to realise that my internship opportunity was greater beyond my expectation, instead maximising my gaining and learning where possible.

What lessons were the most important from your internship? Why were they important?

I was lucky enough to encounter and to still encounter many lessons during my time as an intern. The most important lessons I believe relate to the importance of ownership and team engagement. Being responsible for deployments and management of infrastructure gives a unique insight into the performance of a team and the impact team dynamics can have on the success of work undertaken, and the performance of the team. I have seen first-hand the impact that a lack of communication and teamwork can have on a product, requiring additional intervention prior to its release. This leads to a reduction in profitable output, delays in work completion and creates additional problems in the production pipeline. The software development lifecycle must be adhered to with care, and I have learnt the importance of doing so through the work that I manage and undertake at both face-value and core-value.

Understanding and adhering to the development lifecycle not only ensures fluidity of cooperation in work, but also ensures ownership is taken over each element of work completed. Teams and individual employees can identify work needing to be completed, as well as identify the degree of expertise required to achieve the specified task. This allows the team to operate without doubting the flow of operations, and complete work to a higher standard without sacrificing quality.

After your workplace experience, what would you say your value proposition would be to an employer? How can you demonstrate this?

This experience in the industry has allowed me to expand and better understand my value proposition towards an employer, a distributed focus between technical skill and soft skills, inclusive of ownership and leadership. I maintain an unbiased view on the work that I conduct making sure that the development lifecycle and pipeline are adhered to, whilst catering for both planned and unforeseen circumstances. My career aim remains unchanged, with this experience fuelling my desire to contribute to work that empowers and improves upon the work that others do. I wish to contribute in a way that both innovates and improves existing solutions, and I am able to do so with my engineering background alongside the workplace experience I am gaining.
